Italian Government Fees

Contributo Unificato — Court Filing Fee

Mandatory Italian government fee for citizenship court cases, including 1948 cases and ATQ filings, charged per claimant. Set by Italy's 2025 Budget Law (Law 207/2024) from January 1, 2025. Paid directly to the Italian court; applies regardless of service provider.

€600

~$650 USD · per applicant

Italian Consular Fee

Mandatory government fee for adult citizenship applications at a consulate. Set by Italy's 2025 Budget Law (Law 207/2024) from January 1, 2025. Paid directly to the Italian consulate. If you apply in Italy instead, the municipality may charge up to €600.

€600

~$650 USD · per applicant

Registration Tax After a Court Judgment (Imposta di Registro)

May be due before a court judgment can be certified as final. The amount and who pays depend on the judgment and the court; the Tribunal of Naples, for example, asks claimants for €100 where legal costs are offset. Your lawyer confirms the amount before payment.

Varies

U.S. Document Amendments (OATS)

If name or date-of-birth discrepancies exist in your vital records, Italian authorities may reject your application. An OATS is a U.S. court order that legally corrects these inconsistencies — common when records contain anglicized names or clerical errors. We have partnered with law firms across the U.S. to offer this service.

Cost Drivers

What makes a case cost more or less

  • The route. Consulate applications cost least. Court cases, including 1948 cases, use the $4,500 legal fee and a €600 court fee per claimant instead of the €600 consular fee.
  • How far back your Italian ancestor is. Each generation adds records to order, apostille and translate.
  • How many people apply together. Immediate family on the same line add $200 each, first cousins $500 each, plus €600 each in Italian government fees.
  • Record problems. Name or date conflicts can need a U.S. court amendment (about $4,500–$7,000); missing records can need research ($75 per hour).
  • Where your hearing is held. A $500 travel fee applies only if a court hearing is outside Piemonte and a virtual hearing is refused.

How much do Italian citizenship lawyers charge?

Our legal fee for an Italian court case, including 1948 maternal-line cases, is $4,500, with the case filed and argued by an Italian-licensed lawyer. Other firms set their own fees, and they vary widely. When you compare quotes, compare the total: the legal fee, the Italian court fee of €600 per claimant, translations ($80 per document with us), record ordering, and any travel or hearing fees. Ask whether government fees are included and which Italian lawyer, registered with which bar, will sign your petition.
