Certified Translations
for Italian Citizenship

Which records need an Italian translation, what a certified translation must include, and how consulate and court rules differ.

Last updated: November 7, 2026

Written by Citizenship Italia · Reviewed by: Aldo Ponte, Head of Citizenship Procurement (law degree, University of Turin) Sources: Law 91/1992 · Italian Foreign Ministry Last reviewed: November 7, 2026

Short answer (as of November 2026)

Every U.S. birth, marriage and death record in your line needs an Italian translation, done by a professional translator who signs a statement that it is accurate and complete. Consulates set their own rules for how translations are certified and whether other records need one, so check your consulate's checklist first. For court cases, translations are usually sworn in Italy and arranged by your Italian lawyer.

  • Always translated: birth, marriage, death and divorce records in the line
  • Sometimes translated: naturalization records and other documents, depending on your consulate
  • Order of work: certified copy, then apostille, then translation
  • Machine or amateur translations are rejected

What a certified translation must include

  • A complete Italian translation of the whole document, including stamps, seals and handwritten notes
  • The translator's signed statement that the translation is complete and accurate
  • The translator's name, contact details and, where available, professional credentials
  • A clear link to the source document, usually attached behind the translation

Some consulates require more, for example a notarized translator statement or a specific declaration form. Your consulate's checklist says which.

The right order: certified copy, apostille, translation

  1. Order a certified copy from the vital records office, in the long form your consulate asks for.
  2. Get the apostille from the Secretary of State of the state that issued it. See apostilles for Italian citizenship.
  3. Translate last, so the translator works from the final document, apostille included, if your consulate wants the apostille translated too.

Consulate vs court translations

For the consulate route, a professional translator in the United States can usually do the work, following your consulate's rules. For the court route, translations are usually sworn (asseverate) before an Italian court office or certified in the way the court requires. If you go to court with Citizenship Italia, your Italian lawyer's team arranges this.
