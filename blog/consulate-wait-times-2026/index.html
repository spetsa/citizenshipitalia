Italian Consulate Wait Times 2026: What to Expect by Jurisdiction

How long are citizenship appointments actually taking at Italian consulates in the US — and what are your options if your local consulate is years out?

Published: April 11, 2026 · Updated: November 7, 2026 · By Citizenship Italia

Short answer (as of November 2026)

Italian consulates in the U.S. do not publish official wait times, and waits vary by office and applicant. Consulates keep processing adult applications under annual caps until December 31, 2028; from January 1, 2029, those applications move to a central office in Rome with a 36-month deadline.

Note: These estimates are based on community reporting and client experience as of April 2026. Official wait times are not published by Italian consulates. Jurisdictions follow the Embassy of Italy's official list; Washington, DC (Embassy consular office) covers DC and nearby Maryland and Virginia counties. Actual timelines vary and can change. Contact your consulate or book a consultation with us to get current guidance for your jurisdiction.

Why consulate backlogs keep growing

Italian citizenship applications have surged over the past decade. With Italian ancestry spread across millions of Americans, demand for appointments far exceeds consular capacity. Law 74/2025 has reduced the total eligible population, but the existing backlog — built up over years — has not cleared. High-volume consulates are still working through applications filed years ago, and new appointment slots open slowly.

How to check current availability at your consulate

  1. Find your consulate. It is set by where you legally live, not where your ancestor lived. Each office's area is listed in our consulate guides below.
  2. Check the booking system. Most Italian consulates in the U.S. take citizenship appointments through the Prenot@mi system. New slots are released in batches, so check regularly and at the times your consulate announces.
  3. Read the consulate's own citizenship page. Some offices use waiting lists, email requests or periodic openings instead of open booking. The official page is the only reliable source.
  4. Have your file ready first. If a slot opens, you need complete, apostilled and translated documents on the day. An incomplete file can cost you the appointment.

When do Italian consulates release appointments?

There is no national release calendar. Each consulate decides when to open citizenship slots, how many, and through which channel, and practice changes from year to year. What we see across the U.S. network:

  • Prenot@mi releases come in batches. When a consulate opens new citizenship slots in Prenot@mi, they usually go within minutes. Keep your account active, log in before the announced time, and have your details ready.
  • Watch the consulate's news page. Offices often post the date of a new opening on their website first. That page, not social media rumors, is the reliable signal.
  • Some offices do not use open booking. Some use waiting lists, email requests or applications by mail. The Embassy in Washington, for example, publishes a checklist for applications submitted by mail. Your consulate's citizenship page says which method applies.
  • Openings continue until the 2029 change. Under Law 11/2026, consulates keep handling applications from adults abroad until December 31, 2028, before the work moves to a central office.

Links to each consulate's official citizenship page are in our documents checklist.

Why waits may not shrink before 2029

Under Law 11/2026, each consulate can process no more adult citizenship applications in a year than it did the year before (and no fewer than 100), until December 31, 2028. From January 1, 2029, adults living abroad apply by post to a central Foreign Ministry office in Rome, which has 36 months to decide. If you have an appointment before 2029, keep it. What to do if your consulate is years out

If your consulate's wait time makes the consulate route impractical, you have two meaningful alternatives.

A court action. In some cases, counsel may consider a court action (azione di accertamento, often called ATQ) asking an Italian court to declare your citizenship. It is litigation, not an automatic alternative: the competent court is generally the one for the area where your Italian ancestor was born, whether a delay justifies a court case is assessed case by case, and cases often take 1–3 years. Court cases cost more ($6,000–$7,500 vs. $2,700–$3,100 for a consulate application). Note also that a 2026 law is moving administrative applications from adults abroad to a central office at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, operational from January 1, 2029, which may change consulate wait times.

Apply in Italy. If you're able to establish legal residency in Italy, applying at a local comune (municipality) is often faster, though processing times vary widely by comune. This option requires relocating for the duration of the process, which suits some applicants (particularly those who want to spend time in Italy) but not others.
