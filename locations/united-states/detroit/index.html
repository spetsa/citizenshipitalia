U.S. Consulate Guide

Italian Citizenship by Descent
Through the Italian Consulate in Detroit

Who the Consulate General of Italy in Detroit serves, how to apply under the current rules, and how to prepare records from the states it covers.

Last updated: November 2, 2026

Written by Citizenship Italia · Legal review: Avv. Marta Pignatiello, Italian lawyer Sources: Law 91/1992 · Italian Foreign Ministry Last reviewed: November 2, 2026

Short answer (as of November 2026)

The Consulate General of Italy in Detroit handles citizenship-by-descent applications from people who live in: Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio and Tennessee. Applicants must meet the 2025 Article 3-bis rules, book through the office's online system, and pay €600 per adult. From January 1, 2029, adult applications move to a central office in Rome.

  • Apply where you legally live, not where your ancestor lived or arrived
  • Check eligibility first: most new applications need a parent or grandparent with exclusively Italian citizenship
  • €600 government fee per adult applicant (2025 Budget Law)
  • Official site: consdetroit.esteri.it

Which areas does it cover?

Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio and Tennessee.

Before 1906, naturalizations could take place in any local, state or federal court, so for older cases in Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio and Tennessee the record may sit in a county court rather than with USCIS.

Your consulate is set by your legal residence. You will usually need proof, such as a driver's license or utility bill, that you live in the district. If you move, your file moves to the consulate for your new address.

Not sure which office covers you? Use our consulate finder.

How to apply

  1. Confirm eligibility under Article 3-bis. Unless your case was started by March 27, 2025, you need a parent or grandparent who held exclusively Italian citizenship, or a parent who lived in Italy for two years as a citizen before your birth.
  2. Collect the line documents: birth, marriage and death records for each generation, your ancestor's Italian birth record, and naturalization records or a Certificate of Non-Existence.
  3. Apostille and translate every U.S. record into Italian.
  4. Book an appointment through the office's online booking system (most Italian consulates use Prenot@mi). Check the citizenship page on the official site for the current method and checklist.
  5. Attend and pay the €600 fee per adult applicant as the office instructs.

Before you book or order documents, read the consulate's citizenship by descent page. It has the current checklist, fees and booking method, and it overrides any general guide, including ours. For the full document list, see our 2026 documents checklist.

Preparing documents from your states

Birth, marriage and death records issued by a U.S. state are apostilled by that state's Secretary of State. Federal documents, such as a USCIS Certificate of Non-Existence, are apostilled by the U.S. Department of State.

Records must usually be long-form certified copies. Each consulate publishes its own checklist, so check it before ordering. See our apostille and translation guide.

What changes in 2029

Under Law 11/2026, the Detroit office keeps processing adult applications until December 31, 2028, within an annual cap. From January 1, 2029, adults abroad apply by post to a central Foreign Ministry office in Rome. Minors stay with the consulate. Read more.

Is there an alternative to the consulate?

Yes. An Italian court can recognize citizenship in some cases, such as 1948 maternal-line cases or long administrative delays. Court cases are filed where your Italian ancestor was born, not where you live in the U.S., and require an Italian lawyer. See the court route.
