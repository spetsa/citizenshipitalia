1948 Cases:
Italian Citizenship Through a Female Ancestor

How Italian citizenship can pass from an Italian woman to a child born before January 1, 1948, why these cases go to court, and how they interact with the 2025 law.

Last updated: November 1, 2026

Written by Citizenship Italia · Legal review: Avv. Marta Pignatiello, Italian lawyer Sources: Law 91/1992 · Italian Foreign Ministry Last reviewed: November 1, 2026

At a glance (updated November 2026)

  • A 1948 case arises where Italian citizenship must pass from an Italian woman to a child born before January 1, 1948
  • Under the 1912 law, citizenship passed to children only through the father; Italy's Constitutional Court later struck this down as discriminatory
  • Consulates do not recognize this kind of transmission, so 1948 cases are decided by Italian courts
  • For new cases, you must also meet the 2025 Article 3-bis rules
  • Typical timeline: often 1–3 years; see our pricing page for costs, plus a €600 court fee per claimant

What is a 1948 case?

A "1948 case" arises when your line to Italian citizenship passes through an Italian woman, and the next person in the line (her child) was born before January 1, 1948, the date Italy's Constitution took effect.

Example: Francesca is born in Naples in 1890 and emigrates to the United States. Her daughter Mary is born in New York in 1915. Under the 1912 law, Mary did not receive Italian citizenship from her mother, because citizenship passed only through the father. Mary's descendants therefore cannot be recognized at a consulate through Francesca. An Italian court, however, can recognize the transmission.

Why does 1948 matter?

Law 555/1912, Italy's citizenship law until 1992, made a child Italian at birth through the father. A mother could pass citizenship only in limited cases, such as when the father was unknown or stateless. Separately, some Italian women lost their own citizenship when a foreign husband's nationality was conferred on them by marriage.

Italy's Constitutional Court struck down both rules as discriminatory: the loss-by-marriage rule in Judgment 87/1975, and the father-only transmission rule in Judgment 30/1983. Consulates apply these rulings only to children born on or after January 1, 1948. Since 2009, Italy's Court of Cassation has held that the discrimination can be remedied for children born before that date too, which is why these cases go to court.

These are two different issues. Many 1948 cases involve a woman who never lost her own citizenship but could not pass it on; others involve loss by marriage as well. Your attorney will identify which applies.

Who may have a 1948 case?

You may have a 1948 case if all of the following are true:

  • Your line passes through an Italian woman who was an Italian citizen when her child was born
  • That child (the next person in your line) was born before January 1, 1948
  • The rest of the line down to you is unbroken (for example, no one naturalized abroad before the next person was born)
  • You meet the 2025 Article 3-bis rules, or your case was filed in court by March 27, 2025 (see below)

How does a 1948 case interact with Law 74/2025?

For cases filed after March 27, 2025, Article 3-bis also applies. If you were born abroad and hold another citizenship, a parent or grandparent of yours must hold, or have held at death, exclusively Italian citizenship, or a parent must have lived in Italy for two continuous years after becoming a citizen and before your birth.

The qualifying parent or grandparent is not necessarily the same person as the woman at the center of the 1948 argument. In many U.S. families, the Italian woman is a great-grandmother or further back, and the closer generations hold U.S. citizenship, which makes Article 3-bis hard to meet. Cases filed in court by 11:59 PM Rome time on March 27, 2025 are assessed under the old rules. Each lineage needs individual review.

The court procedure for 1948 cases

Step 1: Legal review and an Italian attorney

You must be represented by a licensed Italian attorney (avvocato). The Italian attorneys we work with review your lineage, including the Article 3-bis question, before anything is filed.

Step 2: Gather and translate documents

Vital records, naturalization records and any other evidence are collected, apostilled and translated into Italian by certified translators.

Step 3: File the petition

For applicants living abroad, the petition is filed with the specialized immigration and citizenship section of the court for the area where the Italian ancestor was born (the rule since June 22, 2022). Law 74/2025 did not move these cases to Rome.

Step 4: Government response and hearing

The Ministry of the Interior is notified and may oppose the claim. The court then decides; hearings may be held in writing, so you usually don't need to attend.

Step 5: Appeal (if necessary)

Either side can appeal. Your attorney will advise whether an appeal is worthwhile.

Documents required for a 1948 case

  • Your documents: birth certificate, and marriage or divorce records if applicable
  • Each generation between you and the Italian woman: birth, marriage and death certificates
  • The Italian woman: Italian birth certificate, marriage and death certificates
  • Her spouse: birth, marriage, death and naturalization records (if applicable)
  • Naturalization records (or Certificates of Non-Existence) for relevant ancestors
  • Proof for Article 3-bis, where it applies (for example, that a parent or grandparent held exclusively Italian citizenship)

All non-Italian documents must be apostilled and translated into Italian by certified translators. Your attorney will confirm the exact list.

Timeline and likelihood of success

1948 cases often take 1–3 years from filing to judgment, depending on the competent court, the complexity of the lineage and whether there is an appeal. At least one court has also postponed a post-reform case while the EU Court of Justice reviews Law 74/2025 (case C-816/26).

The legal principle behind 1948 cases is well established in Italian case law. Outcomes still depend on the facts, the completeness of the evidence and, for new cases, Article 3-bis. Your attorney will give you a candid assessment before you commit.

After a favorable judgment

Once the judgment is final, it is sent to the relevant Italian municipality for registration. You then register with the consulate for your area (AIRE) and can apply for an Italian passport.

Can I try the consulate first?

A consulate will not recognize transmission through a woman to a child born before 1948, so a consulate application in a 1948 case will be refused. Going directly to court is usually more efficient.
