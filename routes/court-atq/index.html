The Italian Citizenship Court Route (ATQ): When a Court Case Makes Sense

A court action (azione di accertamento dello stato di cittadino italiano), often called "Against the Queue," asks an Italian court to declare your citizenship. Here is when counsel may recommend it, which court hears it, and what to expect.

Last updated: November 1, 2026

Written by Citizenship Italia · Reviewed by: Aldo Ponte, Head of Citizenship Procurement (law degree, University of Turin) Sources: Law 91/1992 · Italian Foreign Ministry Last reviewed: November 1, 2026

Short answer (as of November 2026)

An Italian court can recognize citizenship by descent where the consulate route cannot deliver recognition in reasonable time, and in 1948 cases. Since 2022, applicants abroad file in the court for the area where their Italian ancestor was born; Law 74/2025 did not move cases to Rome. New cases must meet Article 3-bis, and outcomes are not guaranteed.

What is a court action for citizenship, and when is it used?

The azione di accertamento dello stato di cittadino italiano (literally, "action for ascertainment of Italian citizen status") is a civil court procedure in which an Italian court is asked to declare that you are an Italian citizen. In English-speaking communities it is often called "Against the Queue" (ATQ), because it has been used where applicants could not get a consulate appointment within a reasonable time.

It is litigation, not a paid fast track. Counsel may consider it where the administrative route (consulate or municipality) cannot provide recognition within a reasonable time, or where a case needs a court's decision, such as a 1948 case. A favorable judgment has the same effect as administrative recognition: your citizenship is registered with the relevant Italian municipality and you can apply for a passport.

Who may be able to bring one?

Two separate questions decide whether a court action is realistic:

1. Are you eligible under current law? You must have an unbroken Italian citizenship line and, for new cases, meet Article 3-bis: a parent or grandparent who holds, or held at death, exclusively Italian citizenship, or a parent who lived in Italy for two continuous years after becoming a citizen and before your birth. Cases filed in court by 11:59 PM Rome time on March 27, 2025 are assessed under the old rules.

2. Is there a basis for going to court? Where the argument is administrative delay, the court must accept that you could not obtain recognition within a reasonable time. Courts have accepted this in some high-backlog jurisdictions, but there is no universal threshold, and the competent court's approach matters. A 2026 law also sets a maximum of 36 months for adult applications received from 2026 and, from January 1, 2029, moves applications from adults abroad to a central office at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs; how courts weigh delay arguments under this new system is still developing. A licensed Italian attorney should assess this for your specific situation before filing.

Which court hears the case?

Since June 22, 2022, when the applicant lives abroad, the case is heard by the specialized immigration and citizenship section of the court for the area where the Italian parent or ancestor was born. Law 74/2025 did not change this or centralize cases in Rome. Your attorney confirms the competent court before filing. You do not need to be in Italy for the proceedings; your attorney represents you.

Documents required for a court case

The document package is substantially the same as for a consulate application, but documents are submitted to a court, so translation and authentication standards are stricter. You will typically need:

  • Birth, marriage, and death certificates for every person in the direct Italian lineage, from your Italian ancestor to you
  • Naturalization records (or a Certificate of Non-Existence) showing when, if ever, each relevant ancestor naturalized
  • Proof that the qualifying parent or grandparent held exclusively Italian citizenship (for example, certificates confirming no other citizenship was held), where Article 3-bis applies
  • Your own long-form birth certificate
  • Apostilles on all non-Italian documents
  • Sworn Italian translations of all non-Italian documents
  • Italian civil records (birth, marriage, death certificates) from the relevant comune

Timeline: what to expect

Court cases often take 1–3 years from filing, depending on the competent court's caseload, the completeness of the document package, and whether issues arise during the proceeding. At least one court has also postponed a post-reform case while the EU Court of Justice reviews Law 74/2025 (case C-816/26). Your attorney will give you an estimate for your specific court.

The Minor Issue and court cases

In July 2026, Italy's Court of Cassation (Joint Sections, Judgment 24045/2026) held that an Italian parent's naturalization did not by itself strip citizenship from a minor child who held another citizenship from birth. Many lineages that were previously refused on Minor Issue grounds may now be handled administratively, and some past denials can be reconsidered. A court case may still be relevant where the facts are unusual. Discuss your specific timeline with your attorney before filing.

What happens after the court judgment

After the court issues a judgment recognizing your Italian citizenship, the following steps occur:

  1. The judgment becomes final and is transmitted to the relevant Italian comune (municipality) for registration in the civil registry
  2. Your Italian citizenship is formally registered — you are now an Italian citizen of record
  3. You register with the consulate for your area (AIRE) and apply for an Italian passport, at the consulate or in Italy
  4. Registration and passport timing vary by municipality and consulate
  5. A registration tax (imposta di registro) may also be due after a court judgment, before the judgment can be certified as final. The amount and who pays depend on the judgment and the court: the Tribunal of Naples, for example, asks claimants to pay €100 where legal costs are offset between the parties. Your lawyer confirms the amount before anything is paid.
