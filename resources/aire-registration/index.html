AIRE Registration Explained

The register of Italians living abroad: why it matters, how to register on Fast It and how to keep your record up to date.

Last updated: November 8, 2026

Written by Citizenship Italia · Reviewed by: Aldo Ponte, Head of Citizenship Procurement (law degree, University of Turin) Sources: Law 91/1992 · Italian Foreign Ministry Last reviewed: November 8, 2026

Short answer (as of November 2026)

AIRE (Anagrafe degli Italiani Residenti all'Estero) is Italy's register of citizens who live abroad. If you are an Italian citizen living in the U.S. for more than 12 months, registering is a legal obligation. It is free and done online on the Fast It portal. You need it for a passport, to vote by mail and for most consular services, and since 2024 failing to register can bring a fine of €200 to €1,000 for each year.

  • Required for Italian citizens who live abroad for more than 12 months
  • Free, and done online on Fast It; creating an account alone does not register you
  • Needed for passports, voting by mail and most consular services
  • Report a move within 90 days, including a move to another consular district
  • Since 2024: fines of €200 to €1,000 for each year you should have been registered, for up to five years

What is AIRE?

AIRE stands for Anagrafe degli Italiani Residenti all'Estero, the register of Italians resident abroad. It records your address abroad, your family and your civil status. Your record is held by an Italian comune and managed through the consulate for the area where you live. Being in AIRE also means you are not on the register of people living in Italy.

Who has to register

Italian citizens who live abroad for more than 12 months must register with the consulate for their area, within 90 days of moving. That includes Americans recognized as Italian by descent, who in most cases have always lived in the U.S. Stays abroad of less than 12 months and seasonal work do not count.

AIRE after recognition

How you were recognizedWhat happens with AIRE
At a U.S. consulateThe consulate usually registers you in AIRE when it completes your case. Log in to Fast It and check that your record shows your current address.
By an Italian court (for example a 1948 case)Once the comune has registered the judgment, register yourself with your consulate on Fast It.
In Italy, while living thereYou are registered as a resident of your comune. If you then move to the U.S. for more than 12 months, register in AIRE within 90 days.

How to register on Fast It

  1. Create an account on Fast It, the Foreign Ministry's consular portal. Creating an account does not register you in AIRE by itself.
  2. Start the request to register your residence abroad and enter your details exactly as they appear in your Italian records or passport. Italian records often treat a middle name as part of the first name.
  3. Upload what your consulate asks for. Boston, for example, asks for a copy of your Italian passport if you have one, proof that you live legally in the U.S. (for U.S. citizens, a U.S. passport, plus a naturalization certificate if you naturalized) and proof of address such as a driver's license or utility bill. It accepts PDFs only, not phone photos.
  4. Submit the request. The consulate checks it and forwards it to the comune. Registration counts from the date you submit a complete request. If something is missing you will be asked for it, and an incomplete request is cancelled after 30 days.

What AIRE registration is needed for

  • Italian passports and other travel and identity documents from your consulate
  • Voting by mail in Italian general elections and referendums. Italians abroad also elect members of Parliament for the overseas constituency
  • Consular certificates and services, including registering marriages and births
  • Showing that you live abroad, for any Italian office that asks

Keep your address current. If it is out of date the consulate cannot reach you, and you may not receive your ballot.

Keeping your record up to date

What changedWhat to do
You moved within the same consular districtDeclare the new address on Fast It within 90 days
You moved to another consular districtDeclare the move to the new consulate within 90 days. If you don't, your record can be cancelled because you cannot be found
You married, divorced or had a childReport it to your consulate so the record can be registered in Italy
You moved to ItalyRegister as a resident with your new comune; your AIRE record then closes

What happens if you don't register

Since January 1, 2024, Italy's 2024 budget law (Law 213/2023) has set fines of €200 to €1,000 for each year in which a citizen living abroad fails to register in AIRE, for up to five years. The comune where the citizen is registered decides and issues the fine. The consulate can also register you on its own initiative when it has the information it needs.

AIRE and taxes

AIRE records where you live; it does not by itself decide where you pay tax. Italy generally taxes its tax residents on their worldwide income and non-residents only on income from Italian sources, such as rent from a property in Italy. If you plan to spend long periods in Italy or earn income there, get advice from a tax professional who knows both systems.
