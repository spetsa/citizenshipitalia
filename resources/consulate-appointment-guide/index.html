Consulate Citizenship Appointment:
A Step-by-Step Preparation Guide

Everything you need to know to prepare for and succeed at your Italian consulate citizenship appointment.

Last updated: November 1, 2026

Written by Citizenship Italia Legal Team · Citizenship Italia Last updated: November 1, 2026

Quick checklist

  • Confirm you meet the current rules (Law 74/2025, Article 3-bis) before your appointment
  • Download your consulate's own document checklist and follow it exactly
  • Every foreign document needs an apostille and an official Italian translation
  • Bring the €600 government fee per adult applicant, in the form your consulate accepts
  • The appointment is for submitting your file; the decision comes later, after the consulate reviews it
  • After recognition you are registered in AIRE (Italians resident abroad) and can apply for a passport

Booking your appointment

Most Italian consulates in the U.S. book citizenship appointments through their online system (usually Prenot@mi), and each consulate sets its own procedure. Waits have often run to several years at the busiest offices. Check your consulate's website for current booking rules, and see our guide to U.S. consular jurisdictions to confirm which office covers your state.

Note that a 2026 law will move recognition applications from adults living abroad to a central office at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, due to be operational from January 1, 2029. Until then, consulates continue to handle applications.

Documents to bring

Your consulate publishes its own checklist, and it is the one that counts. In general, you will need:

For yourself (the applicant):

  • Your long-form birth certificate, apostilled and translated into Italian
  • Your marriage certificate and any divorce decrees, if applicable, apostilled and translated
  • A valid passport or ID and proof of residence in the consulate's jurisdiction
  • The consulate's application form, completed and signed

For each person in your line, back to your Italian ancestor:

  • Birth, marriage and death certificates (Italian records come from the comune; foreign records must be apostilled and translated)
  • Naturalization records, or a Certificate of Non-Existence, showing when, if ever, the Italian ancestor became a citizen of another country

Since 2025, also:

  • Proof that you meet Article 3-bis — for example, that a parent or grandparent held exclusively Italian citizenship (certificates confirming no other citizenship, renunciation records, or certificates of non-registration on foreign electoral rolls), or a historical residence certificate for the two-year rule
  • The €600 fee per adult applicant

Important notes on documents:

  • Apostilles: U.S. vital records need an apostille from the state that issued them (or the U.S. Department of State for federal documents).
  • Translations: Foreign documents must be translated into Italian. Some consulates require translations certified in a particular way; check your consulate's instructions.
  • Originals: The certified copies you order from vital records offices are the documents you submit; consulates generally keep them. Keep your own photocopies.
  • Consistency: Names and dates should match across records. Significant discrepancies may need to be corrected before you apply.

What to expect at the appointment

The appointment is mainly administrative. A consular officer checks that your file is complete, that apostilles and translations are in order, and that you are in the right jurisdiction. You sign your application and pay the fee. The officer may ask questions to clarify names, dates or relationships in your documents, but it is not a test of your knowledge of family history.

Consulates do not normally decide your case at the appointment. If something is missing, they may ask you to send it within a set deadline.

Common reasons for follow-up requests or refusal

Missing or inconsistent documents:

The most common issue is a missing record or a name or date discrepancy between records. If a record cannot be found, a Certificate of Non-Existence from the relevant authority may be needed.

Translation or apostille problems:

Missing apostilles, or translations that do not meet the consulate's requirements, will need to be redone.

Not meeting Article 3-bis:

For applications not started by March 27, 2025, the consulate checks the 2025 rules: a parent or grandparent with exclusively Italian citizenship, or a parent who lived in Italy for two continuous years after becoming a citizen.

The Minor Issue:

Consulates used to refuse applications where an ancestor naturalized while the next person in the line was under 21, citing Law 555/1912. Since the Court of Cassation's July 2026 ruling and the Interior Ministry's August 2026 guidance, a child who held another citizenship from birth is generally treated as having kept Italian citizenship. See our Minor Issue guide.

1948 cases:

If citizenship must pass from an Italian woman to a child born before January 1, 1948, the consulate cannot recognize it. These cases go to an Italian court.

Tips for a smooth appointment

  • Organize your file. Put your documents in order — yours first, then each generation back to your Italian ancestor — with a simple family tree on top.
  • Know your file. Be ready to explain any name variations or gaps.
  • Bring copies. Keep a full scanned set at home.
  • Write down any requests. If the officer asks for more documents, note exactly what is needed and by when.
  • Follow up promptly. Send any requested documents before the deadline and keep proof of what you sent.

What happens after approval

When the consulate recognizes your citizenship, it arranges for your birth (and, if applicable, marriage) records to be registered with the relevant Italian municipality and registers you in AIRE, the registry of Italians resident abroad. You can then book a passport appointment at your consulate. Processing times vary by consulate.

How long does it take after the appointment?

It varies by consulate and by how complete your file is. Reviews can take months, and longer if documents are requested or records must be verified with Italian municipalities. For applications filed from January 1, 2026, the law sets a maximum of 36 months to conclude the procedure. Passport issuance after recognition adds further time.
