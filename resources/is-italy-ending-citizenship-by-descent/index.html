Is Italy Ending Citizenship
by Descent?

No, but it is much narrower than it was. Here is what changed, what did not, and what is still pending.

Last updated: November 8, 2026

Written by Citizenship Italia · Legal review: Avv. Marta Pignatiello, Italian lawyer Sources: Law 91/1992 · Italian Foreign Ministry Last reviewed: November 8, 2026

Short answer (as of November 2026)

No. Italy has not abolished citizenship by descent, but in 2025 it narrowed it sharply. Since March 28, 2025, people born abroad who hold another citizenship generally qualify only through a parent or grandparent who held exclusively Italian citizenship, or a parent who lived in Italy for two years as a citizen before their birth. Italy's Constitutional Court upheld the law in April 2026, and the EU Court of Justice is now reviewing it in case C-816/26. The law stays in force while it does.

  • Not abolished: parent and grandparent lines still work if they meet the 2025 test
  • Great-grandparent and older lines generally no longer work for new applications
  • Cases started by 11:59 PM Rome time on March 27, 2025 follow the old rules
  • April 2026: Constitutional Court upheld the law. July 2026: questions sent to the EU Court (C-816/26)
  • The June 2025 referendum was about naturalization, not descent, and it failed

What changed in 2025

Decree-Law 36/2025, in force from March 28, 2025 and converted by Law 74/2025, added Article 3-bis to Italy's citizenship law. Before it, there was no limit on generations: a line could reach back to an Italian ancestor who was alive after Italy's unification in 1861. Now a person born abroad who holds another citizenship is treated as never having acquired Italian citizenship, unless one of these applies:

  • Their case was started by 11:59 PM Rome time on March 27, 2025
  • A parent or grandparent held exclusively Italian citizenship at their birth, or at the ancestor's death if earlier
  • A parent lived in Italy for at least two years in a row as a citizen, after becoming a citizen and before the person's birth

Our Law 74/2025 guide explains each condition.

What did not change

  • Citizenship by descent still exists. Parent and grandparent lines that meet the test are recognized
  • People already recognized remain citizens; the 2025 rules apply to new applications
  • Recognition by descent still has no language test and no requirement to live in Italy
  • Children of Italian citizens can still become Italian, under rules that depend on their age; see registering children by 2029

The referendum myth

Many people heard that Italy "voted on citizenship" in 2025. It did, but not on descent. The referendum of June 8 and 9, 2025 included a question that would have cut the years of legal residence needed for adults from outside the EU to apply for naturalization from 10 to 5. It failed because turnout, about 30.6%, fell short of the required 50%. It had nothing to do with citizenship by descent.

EU references often take well over a year, and nobody can predict the outcome. Until a judgment, the 2025 law applies. Our EU court case tracker is updated as the case moves.

Other changes in 2026

  • Consulate centralization. Under Law 11/2026, consulates keep processing applications from adults abroad under yearly caps until December 31, 2028. From January 1, 2029, those applications go to a central office in Rome. See consulate centralization
  • The Minor Issue. A July 2026 Court of Cassation ruling changed how older cases involving minor children are treated. See the Minor Issue after 2026

If you don't qualify today

  • Gather your records now, so you are ready if the EU Court's ruling changes things. See citizenship through a great-grandparent
  • If a parent or grandparent of yours is recognized, naturalization after legal residence in Italy can be an option, with its own requirements, including Italian at B1 level
  • If your spouse is Italian, you may be able to apply for citizenship by marriage
