Italian Citizenship Through Your Mother
or a Female Ancestor

When citizenship passes through an Italian woman, why the year 1948 matters, and which route applies to your family under the 2025 rules.

Last updated: November 7, 2026

Written by Citizenship Italia · Legal review: Avv. Marta Pignatiello, Italian lawyer Sources: Law 91/1992 · Italian Foreign Ministry Last reviewed: November 7, 2026

Short answer (as of November 2026)

Yes, Italian citizenship can pass through a woman. If the child of your Italian female ancestor was born on or after January 1, 1948, citizenship passed to that child normally, and the case can usually go to a consulate. If that child was born before 1948, consulates will not recognize it, but Italian courts do: that is a 1948 case. Either way, applications filed after March 27, 2025 must also meet the 2025 rule that a parent or grandparent held exclusively Italian citizenship.

  • Child born on or after January 1, 1948: the mother passes citizenship just as a father does, through the consulate route
  • Child born before January 1, 1948: court only, in a 1948 case
  • An Italian woman who married a foreign man before 1948 is often treated by consulates as having lost her citizenship; courts generally recognize that she kept it
  • New applications must also meet Article 3-bis of Law 91/1992, added in 2025

Why the year 1948 matters

Under Italy's old citizenship law (Law 555/1912), a child was Italian through the father. A mother passed citizenship only in narrow cases, such as when the father was unknown or stateless. Italy's Republican Constitution took effect on January 1, 1948 and made men and women equal before the law.

In Judgment 30/1983 the Constitutional Court struck down the father-only rule. Italian offices apply that ruling only to children born on or after January 1, 1948, the day the Constitution took effect. In 2009 the Court of Cassation (United Sections, Judgment 4466/2009) held that children born before 1948 are also entitled to recognition, but only a judge can grant it. That is why a line through a woman whose child was born before 1948 goes to court.

The court route is filed in Italy by an Italian lawyer. You do not need to travel or live in Italy. See court vs consulate for how the two routes compare.

How the 2025 law applies to female lines

Since March 28, 2025, every new application, through a man or a woman, must also meet Article 3-bis: a parent or grandparent of the applicant must hold, or have held at death, exclusively Italian citizenship, or a parent must have lived in Italy for two continuous years after becoming a citizen. Applications and court cases filed by 11:59 PM Rome time on March 27, 2025 follow the old rules.

In practice, an Italian-born mother or grandmother who never took another citizenship is often the person who satisfies this test. A line that starts with a great-grandmother usually does not, unless a parent or grandparent in between held only Italian citizenship. See who still qualifies under Law 74/2025.

Worked examples

Grandmother born in Italy, mother born in 1952

Your grandmother was born in Italy and never became a U.S. citizen. Your mother was born in the United States in 1952, and you were born in 1980. Your mother was Italian from birth through your grandmother, and so are you. Your grandmother held only Italian citizenship, so the 2025 test is met. This is a consulate case.

Grandmother born in Italy, father born in 1940

Same grandmother, but your father was born in 1940. Because he was born before 1948, a consulate will not recognize citizenship through your grandmother. An Italian court can, in a 1948 case. Your grandmother can still be the qualifying grandparent under the 2025 rules. See what a 1948 case costs.

Documents you will need

A female-line case uses the same documents as any other case, with extra attention on the woman at the center of it:

  • Her Italian birth record, and her marriage record
  • Proof of whether, and when, she became a citizen of another country: naturalization records, or a certificate of non-existence if she never naturalized
  • Birth and marriage records for each generation down to you, apostilled and translated into Italian
