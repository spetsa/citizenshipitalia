The Minor Issue After 2026:
What the Cassation Ruling Means for Your Claim

How the Court of Cassation's July 2026 ruling changed the Minor Issue under Law 555/1912, who it helps, and how it interacts with the 2025 law.

Last updated: November 1, 2026

Written by Citizenship Italia · Legal review: Avv. Marta Pignatiello, Italian lawyer Sources: Law 91/1992 · Italian Foreign Ministry Last reviewed: November 1, 2026

At a glance (updated November 2026)

  • The "Minor Issue" asked whether a child lost Italian citizenship when an Italian parent naturalized abroad while the child was a minor (under 21), under Law 555/1912
  • In July 2026, Italy's Court of Cassation (Joint Sections, Judgment 24045/2026) held that the parent's naturalization did not by itself strip citizenship from a child who held another citizenship from birth, such as a child born in the U.S.
  • The Interior Ministry issued guidance in August 2026, and some applications previously denied solely on this ground can be reconsidered
  • It is not automatic: the child's citizenship at birth, any later renunciation, and other facts still matter
  • New applications must also meet the 2025 Article 3-bis rules, which the ruling does not change

What was the Minor Issue?

Under Law 555/1912, Italy's citizenship law until 1992, Italians who voluntarily naturalized in another country lost Italian citizenship. Article 12 of that law also said that minor children living with a parent who lost citizenship became foreigners if they acquired the foreign citizenship.

Following Court of Cassation rulings in 2023 and 2024, the Interior Ministry and Italian consulates applied Article 12 strictly. If an Italian ancestor naturalized while the next person in the line was under 21, applications were refused on the basis that the child had lost Italian citizenship at that moment. Italian courts were divided on whether this was right.

What changed in 2026

The Court of Cassation's Joint Sections resolved the conflict in July 2026 (Judgments 24045/2026 and 24184/2026). The Court held that Article 7 of Law 555/1912, which protects children born abroad who hold another citizenship from birth, prevails over Article 12. In short, an Italian parent's loss of citizenship did not, in itself, cause a dual-national minor child to lose Italian citizenship. Such a child kept Italian citizenship unless they later renounced it as an adult.

The Interior Ministry implemented the ruling in circular no. 65050 of August 10, 2026, which applies only to situations governed by Law 555/1912 and opens the way to re-examining applications refused under the old interpretation. Some consulates (for example, San Francisco) have published reconsideration request forms.

Who does the ruling help?

  • Children born in the U.S. (or another birthright-citizenship country) to an Italian parent who later naturalized while they were minors. They held two citizenships from birth, which is the situation the ruling protects.
  • Applicants refused on Minor Issue grounds, who may be able to request reconsideration.
  • Cases started before March 27, 2025, which are assessed under the pre-2025 rules and no longer face this obstacle in the typical U.S. scenario.

Situations that still need careful review include a child who was not a dual citizen from birth (for example, born in Italy or in a country without birthright citizenship), a child who later renounced Italian citizenship, and lines that also involve a 1948 issue.

How the Minor Issue interacts with the 2025 law

For new applications, the 2025 reform adds a separate test: a parent or grandparent must have held exclusively Italian citizenship at your birth (or at their death), or a parent must have lived in Italy for two years after becoming a citizen. An ancestor who naturalized abroad held another citizenship, so in many new cases the claim is limited by Article 3-bis even though the Minor Issue itself is no longer an obstacle.

In practice, the 2026 ruling matters most for applications started before March 27, 2025, for previously refused applications, and for families whose qualifying parent or grandparent is a different person from the one who naturalized. Former citizens who were born in Italy and lost citizenship under Law 555/1912 may also be able to reacquire it by declaration until December 31, 2027.
