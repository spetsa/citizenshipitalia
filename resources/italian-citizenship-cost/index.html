How Much Does Italian Citizenship Cost?
Lawyer and Service Fees Explained

Every cost of citizenship by descent in 2026: what Italy charges, what lawyers and services charge for, and what you can do yourself.

Last updated: November 7, 2026

Written by Citizenship Italia · Reviewed by: Aldo Ponte, Head of Citizenship Procurement (law degree, University of Turin) Sources: Law 91/1992 · Italian Foreign Ministry Last reviewed: November 7, 2026

Short answer (as of November 2026)

Italian citizenship by descent costs three kinds of money: Italian government fees (€600 per adult at a consulate, €600 per claimant in court), document costs (records, apostilles, translations), and any help you pay for. With Citizenship Italia, typical totals are $2,700–$3,100 for a consulate case and $6,000–$7,800 for a court case, plus government fees.

  • Government fees are the same whoever helps you; they were set by Italy's 2025 Budget Law
  • A lawyer is required for court cases (including 1948 cases), not for consulate applications
  • Documents and translations are the biggest variable: they grow with every generation in your line
  • Check eligibility under the 2025 rules before paying for anything

A registration tax (imposta di registro) may also be due after a court judgment, before the judgment can be certified as final. The amount and who pays depend on the judgment and the court: the Tribunal of Naples, for example, asks claimants to pay €100 where legal costs are offset between the parties. Your lawyer confirms the amount before anything is paid.

2. Document costs

  • Records: birth, marriage and death records for each generation, plus naturalization records or a Certificate of Non-Existence. Offices set their own fees.
  • Apostilles: each state's Secretary of State (or the U.S. Department of State for federal records) charges per document.
  • Translations: every U.S. record must be translated into Italian. We charge $80 per document of up to 4 pages.
  • Corrections: if names or dates conflict across records, a U.S. court amendment can cost about $4,500–$7,000.

Fees vary widely between providers. Compare the total, not the headline: ask what is included, whether government fees are extra, and whether the lawyer is named and registered with an Italian bar. Our guide on how to choose a service lists the questions to ask.
