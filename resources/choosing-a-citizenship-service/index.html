Choosing Help

Concierge, Lawyer or DIY?
How to Choose Italian Citizenship Help

Questions to ask, red flags to avoid, and an honest comparison of doing it yourself, document services, agencies and Italian law firms.

Last updated: November 7, 2026

Written by Citizenship Italia · Reviewed by: Aldo Ponte, Head of Citizenship Procurement (law degree, University of Turin) Sources: Law 91/1992 · Italian Foreign Ministry Last reviewed: November 2, 2026

Short answer (as of November 2026)

Choose a provider that checks your eligibility under the 2025 rules before taking payment, publishes its fees, and uses an Italian-licensed lawyer for any court case. Ask how they assess exclusively Italian citizenship and where your case would be filed. Avoid anyone who guarantees an outcome or a timeline.

  • Since 2025, eligibility is the first question; a good provider screens before you spend on documents
  • Court cases must be filed by an avvocato registered with an Italian bar (Ordine degli Avvocati)
  • Ask for a written fee schedule that separates service fees from Italian government fees
  • Nobody can guarantee a result, a court date or the outcome of the EU Court case

Questions to ask any provider

  1. How will you check my eligibility under Article 3-bis? Listen for the exclusive-citizenship test and naturalization dates, not just "do you have an Italian ancestor".
  2. Who is the lawyer, and where are they registered? For court cases, ask for the avvocato's name and bar (Ordine). You can check it on the Italian bar registers.
  3. Which court would hear my case? Since June 22, 2022, applicants abroad file in the court for the place where their Italian ancestor was born. A provider that says "Rome" for everyone is out of date.
  4. What exactly is included, and what is extra? Records, apostilles, translations, government fees (€600 per adult applicant), travel fees.
  5. What happens if my case is not viable? Do you review before I pay the main fee?
  6. How do you keep up with changes? Ask about Judgment 63/2026, the July 2026 Minor Issue ruling and case C-816/26.

Red flags

  • A guaranteed result, or a guaranteed timeline
  • No screening of naturalization dates before taking payment
  • "Every court case goes to Rome", or no mention of Article 3-bis
  • No named, registered Italian lawyer for court work
  • Fees that are only revealed after you sign
