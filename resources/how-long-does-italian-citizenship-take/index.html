How Long Does Italian Citizenship
by Descent Take?

Documents take months. After that, the wait depends mostly on your route and your consulate. Here are realistic ranges for 2026.

Last updated: November 8, 2026

Written by Citizenship Italia · Reviewed by: Aldo Ponte, Head of Citizenship Procurement (law degree, University of Turin) Sources: Law 91/1992 · Italian Foreign Ministry Last reviewed: November 8, 2026

Short answer (as of November 2026)

Most U.S. families should plan on months to gather documents and then years, not months, if they apply through a consulate. Documents typically take 3 to 6 months. Consulates do not publish wait times; our unofficial 2026 estimates for a first appointment run from about 2 to 4 years (Houston, Detroit, Philadelphia) to 6 to 9 years or more (New York). Court cases in Italy typically take 18 to 36 months from filing to judgment.

  • Documents: typically 3 to 6 months, longer if records are missing or need correcting
  • First consulate appointment: roughly 2 to 9+ years depending on the consulate (unofficial estimates)
  • Court cases (1948 and delay cases): typically 18 to 36 months from filing to judgment
  • Applying while living in Italy: from a few weeks to up to 36 months, depending on the comune
  • From January 1, 2029, adult applications from abroad go to a central office in Rome

These are estimates from community reports and our clients' experience, not official figures: Italian consulates do not publish wait times, and waits change. They measure the time to a first appointment, not to recognition. More detail is in our consulate wait times report, and the consulate finder shows which office covers you.

Why consulate waits are long, and what changes

Demand for appointments has far outrun consular capacity for years, and the backlog has not cleared even though the 2025 law reduced the number of people who qualify. Under Law 11/2026, until December 31, 2028 each consulate can process no more applications from adults than it did the year before (and no fewer than 100 a year). From January 1, 2029, applications from adults abroad go by post to a central office at the Foreign Ministry in Rome, and the law sets a 36-month deadline for recognition procedures for adults abroad.

Court cases

Some cases go to an Italian court instead of a consulate: mainly 1948 cases through a woman whose child was born before 1948, and cases about unreasonable delays. They are filed in the court for the place where your Italian ancestor was born and need an Italian lawyer. They typically take 18 to 36 months from filing to judgment. After that, the judgment has to become final and be registered by the comune, which can add several months. Compare the routes in court vs consulate.

Applying from Italy

If you qualify and move to Italy as a legal resident, you can apply at your comune instead of a consulate. Processing ranges from a few weeks to up to 36 months, depending on the comune. See applying from Italy.

How to keep your timeline short

  • Check eligibility first. The 2025 rules rule out many claims before any money is spent on documents
  • Order your ancestor's Italian birth record early; comuni can take months to reply
  • Get naturalization records or a Certificate of Non-Existence early too
  • Book on Prenot@mi as soon as you are eligible and ready
  • If a court route applies to your family, compare it with your consulate's wait
  • Apply as a family where you can, so shared records are ordered once

After recognition

Once you are recognized, an Italian passport is a separate, much shorter process at your consulate. If you have children under 18, check the declaration deadlines for children, including May 31, 2029 for some families.
