Benefits of Italian Citizenship
for Americans, and the Obligations

What changes in practice when you become Italian, and what Italy expects from its citizens abroad.

Last updated: November 8, 2026

Written by Citizenship Italia · Reviewed by: Aldo Ponte, Head of Citizenship Procurement (law degree, University of Turin) Sources: Law 91/1992 · Italian Foreign Ministry Last reviewed: November 8, 2026

Short answer (as of November 2026)

Italian citizenship makes you a citizen of the European Union. You can live, work and study in Italy and the rest of the EU without a visa, carry an Italian passport and vote in Italian elections from the U.S., and you keep your U.S. citizenship. In return you must register in AIRE and keep your details up to date. Italy generally does not tax non-residents on income earned outside Italy, and it has no compulsory military service.

  • EU citizenship: live, work, study or retire in any EU country without a visa or work permit
  • An Italian passport, valid for 10 years for adults
  • Vote by mail in Italian elections and referendums once registered in AIRE
  • You keep your U.S. citizenship and still use your U.S. passport for U.S. travel
  • Obligations: register in AIRE and keep it current; no military service

The benefits

Live and work anywhere in the EU

Italian citizens are EU citizens. You can move to Italy or any other EU country to live, work, study or retire without a visa or work permit. When you move to another EU country, EU rules also give your close family members, including a spouse who is not an EU citizen, the right to live there with you, subject to the usual conditions.

No 90-day limit

Americans can usually spend up to 90 days in any 180-day period in the Schengen area without a visa. As an Italian citizen traveling on your Italian passport, that limit does not apply to you. In Italy you can stay for as long as you like; in other EU countries, stays of more than three months come with simple conditions, such as working, studying or having enough money to support yourself, and registering locally.

Study at EU rates

EU citizens generally pay the same university fees as local students in other EU countries, which are often far lower than U.S. tuition. Rules vary by country and university, so check before you apply.

An Italian passport

The Italian passport is valid for 10 years for adults and lets you use EU lanes at the border. Its visa-free access is broadly similar to a U.S. passport's, so the real gain is the right to live and work in Europe rather than extra travel.

A vote in Italy

Once you are registered in AIRE, you can vote by mail from the U.S. in Italian general elections and referendums. Italians abroad elect their own members of Parliament for the overseas constituency.

Healthcare if you move to Italy

Public healthcare in Italy is tied to living there. If you move to Italy and register as a resident, you can enroll in the national health service (SSN). While you live in the U.S., keep your U.S. health coverage and travel insurance.

Citizenship for your children

Children of Italian citizens can be Italian too, but since 2025 there are conditions for children born abroad, and some children have to be declared by set deadlines. See registering children by 2029.

The obligations

ObligationWhat it means for you
Register in AIRERequired if you live abroad for more than 12 months. Free. Since 2024, failing to register can be fined €200 to €1,000 a year
Keep your record currentReport moves within 90 days, plus marriages, divorces and births, to your consulate
Replace your passportItalian passports cannot be renewed; apply for a new one when yours expires
TaxesItaly generally taxes tax residents on worldwide income and non-residents only on Italian income. The U.S. taxes its citizens wherever they live
Military serviceNone. Italy suspended compulsory military service from 2005

What does not change

  • You remain a U.S. citizen. The U.S. State Department says acquiring another nationality does not put U.S. citizenship at risk
  • You must still use your U.S. passport to leave and enter the United States
  • Recognition by descent has no requirement to live in Italy or speak Italian
  • Your U.S. tax filing obligations stay the same
