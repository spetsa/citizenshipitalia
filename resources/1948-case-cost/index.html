How Much Does a 1948 Case Cost?
A 2026 Breakdown

What you pay for an Italian court case through a female ancestor: legal fees, Italian government fees, documents and translations, with worked examples.

Last updated: November 7, 2026

Written by Citizenship Italia · Legal review: Avv. Marta Pignatiello, Italian lawyer Sources: Law 91/1992 · Italian Foreign Ministry Last reviewed: November 7, 2026

Short answer (as of November 2026)

With Citizenship Italia, a typical 1948 case for one applicant costs $6,300 to $7,800, plus the Italian court fee of €600 (about $650) per claimant. That covers the $4,500 legal fee for the court case, plus records and translations. Each additional immediate family member adds $200 to our fee and their own €600 court fee.

  • Legal fee: $4,500 for case preparation and representation by an Italian lawyer
  • Court fee (contributo unificato): €600 per claimant, paid to the Italian court
  • Translations: $80 per document; records: $75 per U.S. record and $150 per Italian record if we order them
  • A $500 travel fee applies only if the hearing is outside Piemonte and the judge refuses a virtual hearing

What makes a 1948 case cost more

  • More generations mean more records to order and translate.
  • Name or date conflicts between records may need a U.S. court amendment.
  • Missing records may need genealogical research ($75 per hour).
  • More claimants add $200 or $500 each to our fee and €600 each in court fees, but sharing one line is far cheaper than separate cases.

Check eligibility before you spend money

Since 2025, a 1948 case must also meet Article 3-bis: a parent or grandparent of the applicant must have held exclusively Italian citizenship, unless the case was filed by March 27, 2025. Many lines that were valid 1948 cases before 2025 no longer qualify, so we check this before you order a single record. See how 1948 cases work, with more examples.
